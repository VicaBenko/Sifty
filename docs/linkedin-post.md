## Post (English)

A few weeks ago I joined the AI workshop of the Moshal Program – Israel. I came in wanting to understand which tools people actually build with today. I left having built something.

The project started with the hardest part: finding an idea. Not a clever one — a real one. So I took a problem I have every single day: my gallery is full of photos I'd delete if I could only find them. Date and face search don't help when what you're looking for is "that whiteboard from the course."

That became Sifty: you describe what you want gone, it finds those photos, copies them into a quarantine folder, and deletes only what you approve. Three days, end to end — engine, interface, product.

I also made it a promo video. Nobody asked me to. There is now a commercial, with music, for a product that doesn't charge anyone money. No regrets.

What surprised me most was how far these tools have come. The distance between "an idea" and "a working product" has never been this short, and learning to actually work that way is the part I'm taking with me.

Thank you Ben Rotenberg and Raz, and the whole Moshal Program team, for the push and the guidance.
